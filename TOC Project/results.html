📝 Step 6: Analysis

Which approach is faster?

For unsorted search, Grover's Algorithm is the fastest in terms of query complexity — O(√n) vs O(n) for classical. However, actual simulation time on classical hardware is higher due to quantum circuit overhead.

How does time increase with input size?

Linear (P): Grows linearly — doubling n roughly doubles steps.
Brute Force (NP): Grows quadratically — doubling n quadruples steps.
Quantum: Grows as √n — doubling n increases steps by only ~1.4x.

Does quantum always outperform classical?

Not always in practice. Quantum simulation on classical hardware adds overhead. For small inputs, classical is faster. The quantum advantage becomes significant for very large databases where the O(√n) scaling dominates.

Why or why not?

Quantum computers use superposition to evaluate multiple states simultaneously and amplitude amplification to boost the probability of the correct answer. This gives a provable quadratic speedup. But current quantum hardware has noise and decoherence limitations.

🎯 Step 7: Conclusion

📌 Problem: Searching for an element in an unsorted list.

📌 Best Classical Approach: Linear Search (P-class) with O(n) complexity is the optimal deterministic classical algorithm for unsorted search.

📌 Brute Force (NP-style): The O(n²) exhaustive approach is significantly slower and demonstrates why NP-hard problems are computationally expensive.

📌 Quantum Advantage: Grover's Algorithm achieves O(√n) complexity — a proven quadratic speedup. For a database of 1 million items, classical needs ~500,000 steps on average, while Grover's needs only ~785 iterations.

📌 Key Insight: Quantum computing does not solve NP-complete problems in polynomial time, but provides meaningful speedups for specific problems like unstructured search. The P vs NP question remains open.
